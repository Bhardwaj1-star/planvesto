# Case 9 — Execution Plan What-If and Impact Guidance

## 1. Real-Life Financial Case

The investor has already selected an appropriate strategy.

The investor is now working with the **Execution Plan** and wants to understand what happens if they change the execution numbers or execution choices.

The investor should be able to participate using their own financial numbers and test different execution possibilities.

The system's role is not to dictate one execution choice.

Its role is to explain the consequences of different execution choices.

The core question is:

> **"If I change this part of my Execution Plan, what impact will it have on my goal?"**

---

## 2. Strategy Does Not Have to Change

Case 9 is different from Case 8.

### Case 8 — Strategy Change

The investor changes the strategic approach.

Example:

**Debt Reduction**

↓

Investor chooses an alternative strategy/path.

### Case 9 — Execution Change

The strategy remains the same.

The investor changes how the selected strategy is executed.

Example:

**Goal Funding Strategy**

↓

Investor changes:

- SIP amount;
- lump-sum amount;
- contribution timing;
- existing-asset utilisation;
- contribution escalation;
- or another execution parameter.

Therefore:

> **Changing execution does not necessarily mean changing strategy.**

---

## 3. Investor Participation

The investor can use their actual financial numbers to test execution alternatives.

For example:

### Current Execution

- Goal: ₹50 lakh
- Target: 8 years
- SIP: ₹50,000/month

The investor can test:

### Option A

SIP = ₹60,000/month

### Option B

SIP = ₹40,000/month

### Option C

Deploy ₹5 lakh from an existing asset

### Option D

Increase contributions later through a step-up

The system recalculates the resulting outcome for each option.

The investor can then decide which execution path they want to pursue.

---

## 4. What Should the System Guide?

The system should guide through **impact analysis**, not by simply saying:

> "Do this."

Instead it should explain:

> **"If you make this execution change, this is the expected impact on your goal."**

And:

> **"If you make this alternative change, the resulting impact is different."**

Relevant impacts can include:

- goal funding requirement;
- projected goal value;
- required contribution;
- funding gap or surplus;
- time to goal;
- liquidity;
- cash-flow impact;
- impact on other goals;
- risk implications;
- debt implications where relevant;
- and changes to the Action Plan.

The exact impact dimensions depend on the execution change being tested.

---

## 5. Example — Goal Funding

Suppose:

- Goal = ₹50 lakh
- Horizon = 8 years
- Current execution = ₹50,000/month

The investor tests:

### Scenario 1 — Increase SIP

₹50,000 → ₹60,000/month

The system calculates the resulting change in:

- projected goal corpus;
- funding gap;
- time to target;
- available surplus;
- and other affected goals where relevant.

### Scenario 2 — Reduce SIP

₹50,000 → ₹40,000/month

The system calculates the resulting consequences.

### Scenario 3 — Deploy Existing Asset

Investor deploys ₹5 lakh.

The system calculates how this changes:

- required future contribution;
- goal feasibility;
- remaining liquidity;
- and other affected goals.

The investor can compare the alternatives before deciding what to implement.

---

## 6. Example — Debt-Related Execution

Suppose the selected strategy is **Debt Reduction**.

The investor can test different execution approaches while keeping the same strategic objective.

For example:

- increase EMI;
- make a lump-sum prepayment;
- use a portion of an existing asset;
- change the timing of repayment;
- or use released EMI after debt reduction for another goal.

The system should show the consequences of each execution choice.

Relevant outputs may include:

- remaining principal;
- interest cost;
- repayment timeline;
- released cash flow;
- liquidity impact;
- goal funding impact;
- and downstream Action Plan changes.

The strategy itself does not necessarily change merely because the execution method changes.

---

## 7. The System Is an Impact Guide

The central business principle is:

> **The system guides the investor by showing consequences.**

It should answer questions such as:

- What happens if I increase this contribution?
- What happens if I reduce it?
- What happens if I deploy this asset?
- What happens if I delay this action?
- What happens if I change the timing?
- What happens to my goal?
- What happens to my other goals?
- What happens to liquidity or debt?

The investor then chooses the execution path.

Therefore:

**Investor input**

↓

**What-if calculation**

↓

**Impact explanation**

↓

**Investor decision**

↓

**Execution Plan update**

---

## 8. Current Plan vs Investor What-If

The existing Execution Plan should not be overwritten merely because the investor is experimenting.

The system should distinguish between:

### Current Accepted Execution

The execution currently adopted by the investor.

and

### What-If Scenario

A proposed change being tested by the investor.

The what-if scenario should allow the investor to understand the consequences before adopting the change.

Therefore:

> **Simulation first. Adoption second.**

Once the investor chooses the alternative, the accepted Execution Plan can be updated.

---

## 9. Goal Impact Is the Primary Guidance

The most important question is usually:

> **"What happens to the goal?"**

For an execution change, the system should therefore show the effect on the relevant goal.

Depending on the change, it should also show whole-plan consequences.

For example:

**Increase SIP**

↓

Goal may be achieved earlier or with greater projected surplus.

But:

↓

Available monthly surplus falls.

Therefore:

↓

Other goals may be affected.

The system should make that relationship visible rather than evaluating the goal in isolation.

---

## 10. Whole-Plan Impact

An execution change for one goal can affect the complete financial plan.

For example:

Investor increases Education SIP.

That may:

- reduce available surplus for Retirement;
- reduce liquidity;
- change asset deployment;
- alter debt repayment capacity;
- or affect another goal's feasibility.

Therefore the system should determine whether the execution change has only a **goal-level impact** or a **material whole-plan impact**.

Where the impact is material, the affected parts of the broader plan should be revalidated.

This is consistent with the principles established in Cases 5, 7, and 8.

---

## 11. Execution Plan Is Participatory

The Execution Plan should not be a static instruction sheet.

It should support investor participation.

The investor should be able to change relevant execution inputs and ask:

> **"What if I do this instead?"**

The system then provides the calculated consequences.

This creates an interactive decision environment where the investor can understand the financial effect of different execution choices using their own numbers.

---

## 12. Relationship to Strategy Builder

The boundaries are:

### Strategy Builder

Determines:

> **"Which strategy/path is appropriate?"**

and provides meaningful alternatives.

### Execution What-If

Determines:

> **"What happens if I execute this selected strategy differently?"**

### Action Plan

Determines:

> **"What actions have actually been accepted for implementation?"**

Therefore:

**Strategy Selection**

↓

**Selected Strategy**

↓

**Execution Plan**

↓

**Investor What-If**

↓

**Impact Analysis**

↓

**Investor Choice**

↓

**Accepted Execution**

↓

**Action Plan**

The execution what-if layer should not silently become a strategy-selection engine.

---

## 13. Relationship to Case 8

Case 8 and Case 9 are intentionally separate.

| Case | Investor changes | System response |
|---|---|---|
| Case 8 | Strategy | Trade-off analysis + strategy revalidation |
| Case 9 | Execution | What-if impact analysis + execution revalidation |

### Case 8

> "I don't want this strategy. I want another strategy."

### Case 9

> "I accept the strategy. I want to execute it differently."

This distinction prevents the system from mixing strategic decisions with execution decisions.

---

## 14. Action Plan

When the investor accepts a what-if execution scenario, the Action Plan should be updated.

For example:

### Existing

- SIP ₹50,000/month.

### Investor tests

- SIP ₹60,000/month.

### Investor accepts

The accepted Execution Plan becomes:

- SIP ₹60,000/month.

The Action Plan should then reflect the new accepted action.

If the investor only tests the scenario and does not accept it:

> **The existing Action Plan remains unchanged.**

Completed actions remain historical records.

Only relevant active/future actions should be changed.

---

## 15. Decision Flow

The established conceptual flow is:

**Selected Strategy**

↓

**Current Execution Plan**

↓

**Investor Changes an Execution Parameter**

↓

**What-If Scenario**

↓

**Goal Impact Analysis**

↓

**Whole-Plan Impact Check**

↓

**Investor Decision**

### If rejected

**Current Execution Plan continues.**

### If accepted

**Updated Execution Plan**

↓

**Updated Action Plan**

↓

**Investor Execution**

---

## 16. Established Business Decisions

1. The investor can participate using their own financial numbers.
2. The investor can test changes to their Execution Plan.
3. The investor does not have to change the strategy to change execution.
4. The system's role is to explain the consequences of execution changes.
5. Goal impact is the primary guidance output.
6. Whole-plan impact should also be evaluated where the execution change can materially affect other goals or financial conditions.
7. What-if experimentation should not automatically overwrite the accepted Execution Plan.
8. Simulation should happen before adoption.
9. The investor decides which execution scenario to accept.
10. Once accepted, the Execution Plan and Action Plan should be updated.
11. The system should guide through financial impact rather than simply issuing unexplained instructions.
12. Strategy Builder remains responsible for strategic alternatives; execution what-if remains responsible for execution impact.
13. The exact scenario storage, calculation engine, comparison UI, adoption mechanism, and materiality rules remain subject to implementation validation.

---

## 17. Final Business Principle

> **The investor chooses how to execute. The system shows what each execution choice is likely to do to the goal and, where relevant, to the complete financial plan.**

Therefore:

**Strategy = What approach should be used?**

**Execution = How will the investor implement that approach?**

**What-If = What happens if the investor changes the implementation?**

**Action Plan = What the investor has actually accepted to do.**

---
